Data Story — Dari Data ke Keputusan (Fraud Detection Analysis)

STORY / 01

Distribusi Nilai Transaksi

Risk is not evenly spread across transactions. 94.6% of transactions fall under Rp500 thousand, yet the extreme tail (above Rp50 million) holds only 47 transactions out of 6.3 million, precisely where material risk hides.

View Project →
STORY / 02

Fraud Amount Analysis

Fraud is not always high value. 52.8% of fraud cases occur in transactions under Rp500 thousand, while the fraud rate itself climbs sharply at higher value bands, showing two perspectives on risk that complement rather than contradict each other.

View Project →
STORY / 03

Class Imbalance & the Accuracy Illusion

A model that always predicts 'not fraud' can still show 99.87% accuracy, because only 0.13% of over 6.3 million transactions are actually fraud. Explores why accuracy alone is misleading under severe class imbalance, and why precision and recall matter more.

View Project →
STORY / 04

Transaction Type & Fraud Concentration

Fraud only appears in two of five transaction types: CASH_OUT and TRANSFER, while CASH_IN, PAYMENT, and DEBIT remain entirely clean. Connects this pattern to FATF findings on layering and monetization channels in mobile money ecosystems.

View Project →
STORY / 05

Balance Movement as a Fraud Indicator

Non-fraud transactions show a median balance change of 0, while fraud transactions show a median drop of Rp436,317, with extreme cases draining up to Rp10 million in a single transaction. Ties this pattern to SA 520 analytical procedures.

View Project →
STORY / 06

Correlation Analysis & the Multivariate Approach

No single indicator is sufficient to detect fraud on its own. Explores why fraud must be read through the interaction of multiple signals together (transaction type, balance movement, amount, and timing) rather than any one indicator in isolation.

View Project →
STORY / 07

Feature Selection for Audit Analytics

Walks through which variables were selected for the fraud detection model and why, including the deliberate exclusion of account name identifiers to avoid overfitting on identity rather than behavioral pattern, grounded in SA 520 and the KPMG Trusted AI Framework.

View Project →

Model Evaluation & Risk Application

EVAL / 01

Metrics in a Fintech Context

Explains why precision and recall carry different real world consequences in fintech: precision protects user trust by limiting false alarms, while recall protects against loss by minimizing missed fraud, discussed alongside OJK and FATF regulatory context.

View Project →
EVAL / 02

Confusion Matrix in Practice

Applies the confusion matrix to a model tested on 1.27 million transactions, breaking down true positives, true negatives, false positives, and false negatives, and the audit implications of each, including the precision-recall trade-off as a professional judgment call.

View Project →
EVAL / 03

Fraud Risk Profiling

Distinguishes risk scoring (the model's job) from risk profiling (the auditor's job), showing how raw model scores are interpreted into High, Medium, and Low risk categories to focus audit procedures, grounded in GIAS 2025 and IPPF.

View Project →
EVAL / 04

Risk Segmentation

Two transactions with identical value, type, and an 84% risk score can still land in different risk tiers. Explores how auditors combine contextual red flags, not just the model score, to segment and prioritize transactions for review.

ML Metrics Explained

METRIC / 01

Confusion Matrix

An accessible explainer using a security guard analogy to introduce the four outcomes behind every fraud detection model: true positive, false negative, false positive, and true negative, the source table behind every other evaluation metric.

METRIC / 02

F1 Score

Continues the security guard analogy to show why averaging precision and recall directly can be misleading, and how F1 score penalizes imbalance between the two instead of masking it.

METRIC / 03
